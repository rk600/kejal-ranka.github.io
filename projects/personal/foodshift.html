The Challenge

VIZCON 2026 theme: "How the world lives, thrives, and connects." I wanted to explore the most fundamental human activity — food — and ask: why do countries eat what they eat? Is it economics? Culture? Geography? And can we predict what they'll eat next?

Key Discoveries

  • China's plate shifted 81 percentage points in 63 years — the fastest major transformation in history. Switzerland moved 11.
  • GDP explains only 34% of meat consumption. The S-curve model (logistic fit) predicts most countries — but India breaks it (eats 1.6% vs predicted 7.5%).
  • Spice is the only food group diverging globally. Everything else converges — plates are becoming more similar worldwide. But spice usage stays fiercely local.
  • The 2010s accelerated dietary change by 45% compared to every previous decade.
  • Every country has a "breakpoint year" — the moment its plate transformed fastest. China: 1984. Korea: 1976. India: 2009.

The Analytical Approach

Data Pipeline

Merged FAO Historic (1961-2013) + Modern (2010-2023) datasets. Mapped 120 food items into 10 canonical groups. Calculated GDP per capita from World Bank total GDP ÷ FAO population. 6 reproducible Python scripts.

ML & Statistical Models

  • K-Means Clustering: 30 features per country → 4 food archetypes (Rapid Carnivores, Grain Guardians, Dairy Heritage, Root Shifters)
  • Logistic S-Curve: Meat % vs log(GDP/capita). R²=0.34 — the LOW fit IS the insight.
  • Culture Score: Residual from S-curve = cultural resistance. Mongolia +14.8, India -4.5.
  • Linear Forecasting: Per country × per food group trend extrapolation to 2035 with 95% CI.
  • Breakpoint Detection: Sliding 10-year window identifying year of maximum transformation.

GenAI Usage

AI (Claude via Kiro CLI) was used throughout: data pipeline code generation, ML model design, insight discovery, narrative writing, and all visualization code. Estimated 70% time reduction. Full project built in one weekend sprint.

The closing insight: "34% of what you eat is determined by your income. 66% is determined by who you are. The plate doesn't show what you eat — it shows who you are."
